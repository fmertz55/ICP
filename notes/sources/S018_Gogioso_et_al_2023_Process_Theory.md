# S018 — Gogioso et al. (2023), Constructor Theory as Process Theory

## Bibliographic record

- Citation key: `GogiosoEtAl2023ProcessTheory`
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.4204/EPTCS.397.9>
- Status: peer-reviewed formal proceedings article
- Stratum: constructor theory; independent formalization

## Why included

Provides an independent categorical formalization and a direct test of whether
constructor-theory task language adds content beyond an ordinary process theory.

## Native result and formalism

Conceivable tasks are relations over attributes, while possible tasks are
induced by constructors within a chosen reference process theory. The possible
tasks form a symmetric monoidal subcategory of relations. Attribute-level
coarse-graining is represented explicitly.

## Common-schema mapping

- `H`: absent from the basic categorical construction.
- `M`: constructor attribute preserved by a task-inducing process.
- `Pi`: processes admitted by the reference theory.
- Future object: relational output attributes.
- Claim type: modal-process formalization.

## Assumptions, limitations, and rival reading

The reference process theory, state sets, substrates, and coarse attributes are
inputs. Individual constructor identity under reuse or simultaneous use remains
open. The authors do not claim that constructor theory is merely redundant, but
their embedding yields a strong ICP null: without added restrictive principles,
the task language may be an exact redescription of ordinary processes.
