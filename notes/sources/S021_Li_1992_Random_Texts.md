# S021 — Li (1992), Random Texts Exhibit Zipf's-Law-Like Word Frequency Distribution

## Bibliographic record

- Citation key: `Li1992RandomTexts`
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1109/18.165464>
- Status: peer-reviewed theoretical note
- Stratum: Zipf mechanisms; random-typing null

## Why included

This is the decisive memory-free counterexample to the inference “Zipf implies
historical or semantic constraint.”

## Native result

Independent random characters, including a delimiter, yield geometrically
decreasing string probabilities while the number of possible strings increases
exponentially with length. Their combination produces a Zipf-law-like relation
for word-length classes.

## Limitations and rival reading

The short derivation is not an exact empirical model of individual word ranks.
Equiprobable letters produce length plateaus and finite-sample staircases. S022
shows that tested random-text variants fail stronger real-text comparisons.
