# S014 — Ge et al. (2012), Maximum Caliber

## Bibliographic record

- Citation key: `GeEtAl2012MaximumCaliber`
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3681941>
- Status: peer-reviewed theoretical article
- Stratum: path ensembles and statistical emergence

## Why included

Provides a clean example of constructing an entire trajectory distribution
from stated constraints—and therefore a key warning about inferential versus
physical constraint.

## Native result and formalism

Maximizing finite-path entropy under singlet constraints yields an independent
process; adding pair-transition constraints yields a Markov process. The
selected constraints determine the maximum-caliber path law.

## Common-schema mapping

- `H`: path observations used to estimate constraints.
- `M`: no necessary material carrier; constraint values enter the inference.
- `Pi`: not a physical intervention set in the derivation.
- Future object: probability law over finite discrete trajectories.
- Claim type: inferential path ensemble.

## Assumptions, limitations, and rival reading

The result depends on a discrete state space, finite paths, and the statistics
the analyst chooses to constrain. Maximum Caliber does not establish that those
statistics are embodied mechanisms that cause future behavior.
