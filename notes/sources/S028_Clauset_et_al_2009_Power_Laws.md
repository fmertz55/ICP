# S028 — Clauset, Shalizi, and Newman (2009), Power-Law Distributions in Empirical Data

- Status: peer-reviewed statistical methods article
- Stratum: Zipf and power-law inference

## Why included

Primary gatekeeper against visual straight-line and regression-based power-law
claims.

## Native result

The framework combines maximum-likelihood estimation, KS-based cutoff
selection, fitted-model bootstrap goodness of fit, and likelihood-ratio
comparisons. Simulations show that familiar log-log least squares can be badly
biased and that finite samples may not distinguish power law from lognormal or
other heavy tails.

## Common-schema mapping

This is an inference method, not a physical mechanism. It determines whether a
declared distribution remains plausible on a stated support before causal
interpretation begins.

## Limitations and rival reading

The ordinary likelihood-ratio variance calculation assumes independent units.
Tokens, ranked counts, sentence pairs, and nested prefixes violate that
assumption unless inference is adapted at document or block level.
