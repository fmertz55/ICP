# S020 — Simon (1955), On a Class of Skew Distribution Functions

## Bibliographic record

- Citation key: `Simon1955Skew`
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/42.3-4.425>
- Status: peer-reviewed theoretical article
- Stratum: Zipf mechanisms; preferential growth

## Why included

Foundational generative mechanism and foundational warning against inferring a
mechanism from its skew marginal.

## Native result

The model combines entry of new classes with growth allocated preferentially
to already populated classes. Simon distinguishes a class-level Assumption I
from the stronger per-type proportional form I-prime. Under its assumptions the
process yields a Yule-family tail. With constant innovation, the expected
number of types grows linearly with sample size.

## Common-schema mapping

- `H`: arrival history and innovation events.
- `M`: current class-count vector plus innovation parameter.
- Future law: probability of a new class and allocation among existing ones.
- ICP status: direct history-to-next-event candidate; embodiment unspecified.

## Limitations and rival reading

The constant-innovation model lacks syntax, semantics, and local successor
constraints. Simon explicitly concludes that observing the distribution says
little about the phenomenon because multiple mechanisms can yield it.
