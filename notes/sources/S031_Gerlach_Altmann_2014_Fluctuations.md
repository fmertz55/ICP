# S031 — Gerlach and Altmann (2014), Scaling Laws and Word-Frequency Fluctuations

## Bibliographic record

- Citation key: `GerlachAltmann2014Fluctuations`
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/16/11/113010>
- Status: peer-reviewed model and empirical analysis
- Stratum: Zipf dependence, document heterogeneity, and fluctuations

## Why included

Shows that matching mean Zipf-Heaps scaling is much weaker than matching
fluctuations and identifies document/topic mixture as a major source.

## Native result

An independent Poisson-Zipf model captures mean vocabulary behavior but
overestimates vocabulary and severely underestimates its variance. Vocabulary
fluctuations are non-self-averaging across documents. A quenched topical model
explains much of the anomaly; within-text token shuffling changes little in the
reported written-text setting.

## Common-schema mapping

- `M`: document/topic condition in the mixture account.
- Future law: conditional word-use and vocabulary growth.
- ICP status: document-level latent rival, not inherited carrier evidence.

## Limitations and rival reading

Results are domain specific. Weak order effects here do not imply weak order
effects in browsing, music, or PSA.
