# S001 — Deutsch (2013), Constructor Theory

## Bibliographic record

- Citation key: `Deutsch2013Constructor`
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-013-0279-z>
- Status: peer-reviewed theoretical article
- Stratum: constructor theory

## Why included

Foundational statement of a physical program formulated through possible and
impossible transformation tasks rather than only trajectories from initial
conditions.

## Native result and formalism

A task specifies input-output transformations of substrate attributes. A task
is possible when a constructor can perform it with arbitrarily high accuracy
and retain the ability to do so again; otherwise it is impossible. Subsidiary
theories supply the physical content of those claims.

## Common-schema mapping

- `H`: not primitive in the foundational formulation.
- `M`: substrate attributes plus an available constructor, not necessarily a
  history-formed memory.
- `Pi`: constructors and resources admitted by the task statement.
- Future object: modal task possibility, not a path distribution.
- Claim type: physical/modal; neither predictive nor counterfactual by itself.

## Assumptions, limitations, and rival reading

Possibility uses a limiting repeatability and accuracy ideal and depends on how
substrates, attributes, and resources are declared. The paper is programmatic:
without a bridge theorem or distinctive test, ordinary physical laws may simply
be redescribed in task language.

## ICP relationship

- Direction/directness: supports / direct for hard can-cannot language;
  qualifies / direct for temporal inheritance.
- What ICP must add: a relation from realized history and embodied record to a
  finite-horizon intervention-response family.
- Break point: a constructor-possible task is not automatically the support of a
  stochastic path law.
